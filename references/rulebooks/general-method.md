# The general method (for everyone outside UK central government and US federal government)

Charities, companies, universities, local and devolved government, international organisations and anyone else are not bound by the Green Book or OMB circulars, but both contain hard-won good practice. This is choosewell's default method for them: the best of the UK and US approaches plus decision-analysis practice, without the parts that only make sense inside a national treasury.

Tell the user this is the default ("Using choosewell's general method, which borrows from UK Treasury and US federal practice; want the reasoning or a stricter version?").

## What we take from each, and why

| Step | Take this | From | Why it's worth keeping |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frame | State the **need without presupposing a solution** | US (GAO analysis of alternatives) | Stops the long list being built around a favourite |
| Frame | **Rationale, theory of change, SMART objectives** framed as outcomes | UK (Green Book) | Gives strategic fit something to test against |
| Frame | **Decision hierarchy** (given / decide now / decide later) and sizing by reversibility and stakes | Decision analysis | Sets depth; finds option value |
| Long list | **Options framework** (scope, solution, delivery, implementation, funding) to build options as coherent combinations | UK | Prevents comparing different versions of the "same" option |
| Long list | Alternatives varying **scale, method of provision and degree of involvement**; always include **status quo / BAU** | US (A-94) and UK | Makes the benchmark explicit |
| Long list | **Flexible versions** (stage, pilot, defer, upgrade, switch, exit) | Real options practice | Uncertainty is cheaper to manage than to predict |
| Criteria | **Critical success factors with strategic fit as a gate**, RAG filter with deal-breakers | UK | Fast, auditable narrowing; fails options for the right reasons |
| Criteria | **Criteria and weights agreed before analysis**; **swing weighting** (not importance) | US (GAO) and UK MCDA guidance | Prevents reverse-engineering; weights reflect real differences |
| Assess | Weighted scoring (MCDA) **at either long- or short-list stage** where monetising isn't practical | Relaxes a UK rule | The UK long-list-only rule exists for Treasury spending control; others needn't follow it |
| Assess | **Monetise where you sensibly can** (net present value or cost-effectiveness), keep cost separate from benefit scores, use **life-cycle cost** | US (A-94, GAO) and UK | Cost is a trade-off, not just another criterion |
| Assess | **Optimism-bias / outside-view adjustment** to costs, times and benefits | UK (and reference-class forecasting) | Plans are systematically too rosy |
| Assess | Adjust for risk through **expected values and scenarios** (`methods/quantified-tree.md`), not by fiddling the discount rate | US (A-94) | Keeps risk visible |
| Check | **Sensitivity and switching values** for the preferred option; scenarios/regret if deeply uncertain | UK and US | "The answer holds unless…" is what decision-makers need |
| Present | **Appraisal summary table** showing all impacts per option, including unmonetised ones | UK | Nothing is dropped just because it can't be priced |
| Present | **One document** recording every step; **independent review** proportionate to stakes | US (GAO) and UK (Aqua Book) | Credibility and a clean audit trail |
| Throughout | **Proportionality** | UK | Effort follows the size of the decision |

## What we drop or replace

| Government-specific element | Replace with |
|---|---|
| Treasury discount rates (UK social time preference rate; US 7% real) | The organisation's own cost of capital or hurdle rate (or a simple undiscounted comparison for short horizons); test sensitivity |
| UK statutory duties (equality, biodiversity, environmental principles) | The organisation's own legal duties and policies (e.g. equality law where it applies, safeguarding, ESG commitments, funder conditions) |
| Managing Public Money, spending powers | Governance and delegated authority rules; funder or investor conditions |
| Five case model as a required document format | Use the five questions as a checklist (strategic, economic, commercial, financial, management), not as a mandatory structure |
| Prohibition on simple scoring | Allowed for quick screens, with a transparency note; recommend swing weights for anything consequential |

## Defaults by depth

- **Quick screen**: need statement, BAU, knock-outs, 3–5 criteria with written levels, equal or swing weights, one-line sensitivity ("would change if…"), sources for every fact.
- **Standard**: plus success-factor gate, independent scoring, value vs cost, switching values, information-value triage, adverse consequences.
- **Full**: plus options framework, life-cycle cost / NPV with outside-view adjustment, scenarios and regret, appraisal summary table, independent review.

## Checklist
- [quick] General method stated as the base (best of UK and US practice)
- [standard] Organisation's own legal duties, policies and funder conditions treated as constraints
- [full] Discounting (if any) at the organisation's cost of capital / hurdle rate, with sensitivity
- [full] Appraisal summary table showing all impacts per option, including unmonetised ones
