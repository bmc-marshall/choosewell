# Sources of methods (for "How we worked" sections)

Use these one-line descriptions when telling readers where a method comes from. Cite the source name; add the link where the output allows. Say plainly when a rule is a choosewell default or our judgement rather than an external standard.

| Method or idea | Where it comes from | One-line description for readers |
|---|---|---|
| Options appraisal steps, critical success factors, long list → short list, business-as-usual benchmark, options framework (scope, solution, delivery, implementation, funding) | HM Treasury, *The Green Book* (2026 edition), gov.uk | The UK government's standard method for appraising options, which also works well outside government |
| Weighted scoring rules, swing weighting, 0–100 scales, "simple scoring and weighting is not compliant", long list only | HM Treasury Green Book supplementary guidance, *Use of Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis in options appraisal of economic cases* (2024); Analysis Function, *An Introductory Guide to MCDA* | UK government guidance on how to score and weight options properly |
| Real options, decision trees, adaptive pathways in appraisal | HM Treasury Green Book supplementary guidance, *Accounting for the effects of climate change* | UK guidance on valuing flexibility when the future is uncertain |
| Analytical quality assurance roles | HM Treasury, *The Aqua Book* | UK guidance on quality-assuring government analysis |
| US federal benefit-cost analysis | OMB Circular A-94 (1992 version, reinstated by memo M-25-23, April 2025); OMB Circular A-4 (2003) | The US government's rules for analysing programmes and regulations |
| Analysis of alternatives best practices | US Government Accountability Office, *Cost Estimating and Assessment Guide* (AOA best practices; GAO-16-22) | The US federal auditor's 22 practices for a credible comparison of options |
| Two-way and one-way door decisions | Jeff Bezos, Amazon shareholder letters (2015, 2016) | Reversible decisions should be made fast; irreversible ones slowly and carefully |
| Cynefin framework | Dave Snowden and Mary Boone, "A Leader's Framework for Decision Making", *Harvard Business Review* (2007) | Different kinds of problem (clear, complicated, complex, chaotic) need different approaches |
| Decision quality chain | Carl Spetzler, Hannah Winter and Jennifer Meyer, *Decision Quality* (2016); Strategic Decisions Group | A decision is only as good as the weakest of six elements: frame, alternatives, information, values, reasoning, commitment |
| Decision hierarchy, strategy tables | Strategic Decisions Group decision-analysis practice | Separating what's already decided, what to decide now and what to decide later |
| Value of information, expected value of perfect information | Decision analysis (Howard Raiffa; Ronald Howard); Douglas Hubbard, *How to Measure Anything* | Information is only worth gathering if it could change the decision, and is worth at most what a wrong decision would cost |
| Decision trees, folding back, expected monetary value, risk profiles, value of sample information, Bayes' rule ("tree flip") | Decision analysis (Howard Raiffa, *Decision Analysis*, 1968; Ronald Howard); standard management-science texts | Lay out choices and uncertain events in time order, weight each outcome by its probability, and work back to the best strategy |
| Exponential utility, risk tolerance, certainty equivalents; risk-tolerance rules of thumb | Ronald Howard, "Decision analysis: practice and promise", *Management Science* (1988) | A way to account for an organisation's appetite for risk with a single number |
| Sequencing pass/fail tests (cost ÷ chance of failure) | Classic result in decision analysis and operations research on ordering inspections and tests | Cheap tests that are likely to fail go first, unless a late failure costs more |
| Dominance, weight ranges, switching values, tornado and random-weight tests for weighted scores | Multi-criteria decision analysis practice: Belton and Stewart, *Multiple Criteria Decision Analysis* (2002); HM Treasury, *Use of MCDA in options appraisal of economic cases* (2024) | Shows how much a ranking depends on particular scores and weights |
| Measurement inversion; calibrated estimates; spending ~10% of EVPI | Douglas Hubbard, *How to Measure Anything* | Organisations often measure what's easy rather than what matters |
| Minimax regret, satisficing, robust decision making, adaptive pathways | Decision theory (Leonard Savage; Herbert Simon); Marchau et al. (eds), *Decision Making under Deep Uncertainty* (2019) | Ways to choose when you can't sensibly put probabilities on the future |
| Musts and wants; adverse consequences | Kepner-Tregoe decision analysis (Kepner and Tregoe, *The Rational Manager*, 1965) | Separate non-negotiable requirements from preferences, then test the leading options for what could go wrong |
| Independent scoring before discussion; delaying overall judgement | Kahneman, Sibony and Sunstein, *Noise* (2021), "mediating assessments protocol" | Structured group judgement that reduces random variation and anchoring |
| Pre-mortem | Gary Klein, "Performing a Project Premortem", *Harvard Business Review* (2007) | Imagine the decision has failed and ask why, before committing |
| Reference-class forecasting, optimism bias | Kahneman and Tversky; Bent Flyvbjerg; Green Book optimism-bias guidance | Estimate from how similar projects actually turned out |
| Eisenhower matrix | Attributed to Dwight Eisenhower; popularised by Stephen Covey | Sort tasks by urgency and importance |
| Responsibility codes (accountable, delivers, supports) | Adapted from RACI responsibility matrices | Who is answerable for, does, or helps with each activity |
| Process framework for activity mapping | APQC Process Classification Framework | A standard catalogue of business activities used as a starting point |
| Activity, operating and transaction models; impact × availability; roll-up thresholds (+0.4, +0.5); six issue types | Choosewell practice, from real appraisals; thresholds calibrated on one working group's ratings | Our own approach: state it as such, and invite the group to adjust it |
