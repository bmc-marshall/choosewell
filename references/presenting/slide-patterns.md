# Slide patterns for appraisals

These are **structural** patterns: what a slide contains and how it's arranged. They deliberately say nothing about colours, fonts or brand: the look comes from the user's template or from slidewell's theme step. Pick the patterns the storyline needs; most appraisals use 8–15 of them.

Each pattern lists: purpose, contents, and notes.

## Framing

**Method overview (numbered steps)**: the steps of the method left to right (e.g. rationale → objectives → long list → short list → analyse → select), each with a short description; on later uses, highlight the steps this pack covers ("scope of this document") and note status per step.

**Principles table**: principle → issue addressed → implication; split "the appraisal must…" / "the selected option must…".

**Three-layer framing**: three columns (activity / operating / transaction models), each with what/who/how, how it's decided, and small examples; the layer this pack covers outlined.

**Scope spectrum**: facets as rows, options as pills marked by level (initial priority / major potential / future), rationale column, legend.

**Key question (SCQA)**: situation, complication, key question, in three boxes.

## Long list and criteria

**Long-list family tree**: families on the left, sub-groups in the middle, numbered options on the right with "e.g." examples; early exclusions as a footnote with reasons.

**Long list by source**: numbered groups (e.g. business priorities, approved list, discovered in research, future screening) with the names in each.

**How we worked**: the steps taken in plain language; for each method, one line on what it is and where it comes from (framework, guidance or research); the choices that were our own judgement; the evidence base.

**Criteria with score definitions**: one row per criterion (full name and the question it asks, weight as a percentage), then columns headed "Score 3 (best)", "Score 2", "Score 1", "Score 0 (worst)" describing what earns each score.

**Criteria and sub-questions**: criteria down the side with their dimension (strategic, economic, commercial, financial, management, local), considerations, and questions to ask; a key for standard vs local criteria.

**Threshold criteria table**: criterion (and why), measure, requirement; "at least one of these" bracket where used.

**Roll-up rules**: legend for each rating (e.g. Green = preferred way forward; Amber = carry forward; Red = drop) and the aggregation principles; "based on qualitative assessment by the working group".

**Scoring logic**: definitions of each axis or scale level with the grid; for matrices, the impact metrics and availability factors.

## Assessment

**Indicator matrix (one per criterion)**: headline = the criterion's question; indicators down the side; options across (rotated labels if many); ✓ / – / ✗ / ? in cells; criterion rating as a labelled bar above each option; setup/ongoing marker; key questions in notes.

**Summary RAG matrix**: options grouped by family × criteria, then overall and "progress to short list"; split ratings shown as split cells with both letters.

**Ranked summary table**: type tag, name, key facts, total score, per-criterion scores; tier separators; note on unknowns and knock-outs.

**Stars grid**: options × criteria with 1–3 stars, criteria definitions alongside, the selected set outlined.

**Funnel with counts**: selection steps and the number remaining after each; shortlist named; pre-exclusions footnoted; paired with a map or grid of candidates coloured by status.

**Narrowing waterfall**: starting count, additions, each exclusion with its rule, final count split (e.g. new vs existing).

**Plotted matrix**: grid with counts per cell, zones labelled, key priorities listed alongside, exclusion note.

**Responsibility heatmap**: organisations × codes (A / AD / D / S) with counts, callouts summarising patterns.

**Issue-type table**: issue → example as-is → action → example to-be.

**Decision tree**: questions on the left ordered by when answered, options on the right, yes/no paths, red lines marked, "further considerations" box with sub-questions; staged versions for milestones.

**Requirement × place matrix**: requirements as rows, places as columns, ✓ / ✗ with short text; key definitions on a preceding slide; disclaimer.

**Comparator table / tick matrix**: comparators as columns, attributes as rows; "notes for us" row.

## Results

**Drop-out tracker**: options down the side grouped by family; stages across the top (initial sift → strategic fit → other criteria → overall → short list); a line per option ending in × where it drops out; survivors named at the right; preferred option outlined. A compact, high-trust summary of the whole process.

**In-house vs new vehicle (basic choice)**: two groups of options, what each side offers, "vs" between; nuance note for intermediate options.

**Rationale vs alternatives**: preferred option at the top with ✓; each alternative (or family) below with ×, and "main reasons for X over Y" (three each); residual-risks box at the bottom ("despite its preferred status, this option still presents risks needing management: …").

**Option cards**: two to four columns labelled Preferred / Do minimum / Do nothing / Alternative; one-line description; ✓ pros; ✗ cons; preferred outlined.

**Pros/cons of top tier**: name, pros, cons, one line each.

**Answer on a page**: rows for scope, activity, phasing, operating model, team, funding (and any others), two or three bullets each; "for discussion" tag if not yet agreed.

## Uncertainty and robustness

**Tornado chart**: inputs ranked by how much they swing the result between their low and high values; the preferred option's switching values noted.

**Switching values table**: input, current value, value at which the preferred option changes, and how plausible that is.

**Weight sensitivity**: for each criterion, the range of weights over which the top option stays top.

**Scenario / regret table**: options × scenarios with payoffs or ratings; regret per cell; max regret per option; robust choice highlighted.

**Information-value table**: open questions with "could it change the choice?", stakes if wrong, cost/time to find out, and action (chase / park), with owners.

**Adaptive pathway map**: options as lines over time, switch points and triggers marked; table of triggers, signposts, lead times and owners.

**Decision tree with values**: decision and chance nodes, probabilities, payoffs, rolled-back expected values; rejected branches marked; the strategy in one line underneath; the value of flexibility called out. Prune to the branches that matter and put side calculations in an appendix.

**Risk profile**: for the top two or three options, a bar for each possible result showing its probability, with the expected value, chance of a loss and worst case labelled. Use it beside the tree whenever the options differ in risk.

**Break-even chart**: one uncertain input along the bottom, each option's value as a line, the crossing points labelled ("above 45%, launch at full scale"). Best single slide for a probability nobody agrees on.

**Value of information table**: each study or test considered, what it could tell you, its cost, the most it's worth (EVSI), and the decision rule after each result; EVPI as the ceiling.

**Pre-mortem / adverse consequences**: top two or three options, what could go wrong, probability × severity, mitigations.

**Decision-quality check**: the six links rated, weakest link named.

## Profiles ("X on a page")

Use one consistent template per profile type; the same fields in the same places on every page. Variants:
- **Organisation**: header with type and badges; information box (HQ, founded, revenue and segment revenue, staff, segments); scores with total; evaluation bullets by criterion plus Other; nice-to-haves; next steps; notes with highlights and sources.
- **Place**: summary box; size table; funding or economics chart; political landscape table; programme stances with commentary; sources in notes.
- **Place by theme**: one page per theme (buyer landscape: top organisations and industry mix; staffing costs: roles and costs at thresholds; regulation).
- **Model / option fact sheet**: relationship diagram; headline indicators (staff, set-up time, risk); arrangement table (purpose, commitments, arrangements, liability, suppliers, procurement, revenue); risks in bid / in life; clearance; lessons.

## Closing

**Legal considerations**: categories numbered with example regulations and the questions for advisers; or the phased legal-advice plan.

**Evidence base by stage**: stages down the side, evidence per stage, footnoted sources, iteration arrow.

**Recommendations by horizon / asks**: short, medium, long term; "the board is asked to agree / note / steer".

**Next steps**: owner, action, date; open questions with owners.
