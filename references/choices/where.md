# Type of choice: Where ("Where should we work?")

Use for prioritising countries, states, regions, cities, markets or sites: market entry, expansion, pilots, office locations.

## Default method (choose by situation)

| Situation | Recommend |
|---|---|
| Many candidates (more than about 12), measurable data | **Threshold filter** (`methods/threshold-filter.md`) → shortlist → profiles |
| Few candidates (up to about 8), judgement and mixed evidence | **Stars / scored grid** (`methods/scored-criteria.md`) → preferred places → profiles |
| Both, or in between | A light filter on true requirements only, then a scored grid, then profiles and a recommendation |

Always add a **regulatory comparison** if rules differ by place and affect the model (`topics/legal-regulatory.md` §4); if nobody yet knows whether they differ, log that as an open question with an owner, and consider segment choice first if the "where" depends on "which customers" (below).

## Steps

1. **Frame**: what will happen in the chosen place(s) (sell, deliver, set up an office, pilot), and what makes a place attractive for *that* purpose.
2. **Segment first if needed.** Where you sell depends on who buys: evaluate target segments (e.g. corporates, large private firms, existing clients' local offices, SMEs, public sector) on criteria such as convenience (existing knowledge and relationships), buyer concentration, spending power; or on competition, product fit and market size (sized TAM → SAM → SOM with stated formulas and share assumptions). Then choose places where the chosen segments concentrate.
3. **Long list** of places with pre-exclusions and reasons (existing market, pending contract, competitor exclusivity).
4. **Criteria and measures.** Starters:
   - **Need or impact** (for social purposes: how much need there is and how much difference you'd make), often the main criterion for a public or charitable body
   - **Market size** (customers, institutions, enrolment, companies headquartered)
   - **Budget / spending power** (funding per head, spend per employee)
   - **Accessibility** (size of average buyer; ability to recover acquisition cost)
   - **Convenience** (existing relationships and clients; regulatory ease)
   - **Buyer concentration** (how clustered the target customers are)
   - **Cost** (set-up and running: rent, tax, cost of living, staffing costs)
   - **Political / policy support** (stance of leaders on relevant programmes; legislative control)
   - **Regulatory regime** (licensing, title protection, mutual recognition)
   - **Talent** (availability, education levels)
   - **Cost to serve** (travel, delivery, local partners needed)
   - **Competition** (who's already there, and how strong)
   - **Proximity to current operations** (shared staff, supply lines, time zones)
   If the data has no measure of the main criterion (often need or impact), say so before ranking anything: a ranking on the measures available may answer a different question. If data columns arrive without units or definitions, ask what each means before using it, and log your assumption meanwhile.
5. **Assess** with the chosen method; run sensitivity on thresholds or scores.
6. **Profile the shortlist**: one page per place, or one page per theme per place (buyer landscape; staffing costs; political landscape; regulation).
7. **Recommend**: if two places win on different criteria, recommend both with the reason for each so the decision-maker picks the priority. Where the attractiveness of places depends on uncertain futures (policy, funding, regulation), compare the shortlist across a few scenarios and show regret (which place is least bad if we're wrong: `steps/08-test.md` §8.4). Consider sequencing (enter one place first, with a trigger for the second) as an adaptive pathway.
8. **Cost vs value**: where cost is a criterion in a quick grid, say so; for a fuller analysis, show value against cost separately.

## Profile ("place on a page") fields

- **Summary box** at the top: the verdict in two sentences.
- **Size**: e.g. number of institutions and enrolment by type; or top companies headquartered with revenue, profit, employees, and an industry mix chart.
- **Funding / economics**: e.g. funding per head split by source (stacked bar); staffing cost by role and at revenue thresholds.
- **Political landscape**: executive party, legislative control; stance on relevant programmes (support / neutral / oppose) with one-line commentary each.
- **Regulation**: key requirements and implication.
- **Sources** in the notes.
- Optionally a **"removed from shortlist"** profile for an instructive near-miss.

## Outputs and storyline

Criteria table → funnel with counts (or stars grid) → map of all candidates (shortlisted / longlisted / removed) → regulatory comparison → place profiles → recommendation and next steps (e.g. deep-dive interviews, market sizing per place).

## Worked examples (illustrative: composites with details changed)

- **A service provider expanding beyond its home state** screened the remaining states after excluding a few for known reasons (a contract already pending, a competitor with an exclusive deal). Filters in order: market size (any of three measures above a threshold), funding per user, average site size, and evidence of political support for a relevant programme. Each filter cut the list, ending with five. Each shortlisted state got a one-page profile with sources, and one state that just missed the cut was shown to explain where the line fell.
- **A company entering a new country** first chose which buyers to target, then rated half a dozen cities one to three stars on convenience, concentration of buyers and cost. It recommended two, for different reasons (one cheaper to operate, one with existing clients), and added a regulatory comparison of licensing rules for its specialist staff.

## Checklist
- [standard] Segment chosen before places (if where depends on who buys)
- [standard] Regulatory comparison by place (definitions → requirement matrix → implication → disclaimer)
- [standard] Place profiles (per place or per theme); sequencing as an adaptive pathway considered
