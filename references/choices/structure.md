# Type of choice: Structure ("What form should we take?")

Use when choosing an organisational form, delivery vehicle, host or operating model for a new unit, programme or service: hosted programme vs new agency vs company vs joint venture, and so on.

## Default method

**Green Book-style appraisal with RAG against CSFs** (`rulebooks/green-book.md`, `methods/rag-success-factors.md`), with comparator research to build the long list (`topics/comparators.md`) and a legal module (`topics/legal-regulatory.md`). Sub-choices that follow (team, funding, name, hosting) use `choices/design-choices.md`.

For a quick or private-sector version: same steps, fewer indicators, CSFs renamed as needed (strategic fit, value, delivery partners, affordability, achievability), no CBA.

**How a capability is provided** (build or modernise in-house, buy a commercial product, use a shared or another body's service, outsource) is a structure choice too. Use must-have requirements as knock-outs or a red/amber/green screen, then scored effectiveness criteria with a separate life-cycle cost; in US federal work, follow the analysis-of-alternatives practice in `rulebooks/us-federal.md`.

## Steps

1. **Frame and principles** (process principles: "the appraisal must…"; outcome principles: "the selected option must…"), in the principle → issue → implication format.
2. **Name the layer**: activity / operating / transaction (`steps/01-frame.md` §1.3). This type of choice appraises the operating model (the Green Book's delivery dimension).
3. **Comparators**: how peers at home and abroad structure similar vehicles (model, size, remit, accountability, funding).
4. **Long list** grouped into families. Starter list for public-sector bodies (adapt names to the jurisdiction):
   - *Direct public provision*: existing national body; existing department unit; existing executive agency; other existing arm's-length body; hosted programme / joint initiative across bodies; new executive agency; new non-departmental public body
   - *Public-private partnership*: joint venture; commissioned service (from public or private provider)
   - *Not-for-profit*: community interest company; charity; social organisation (co-operative, community benefit society, mutual)
   - *Company structure*: government-owned company (limited by shares or guarantee, LLP)
   - Plus BAU.
   Record early exclusions (e.g. a form policy currently prohibits, one that could only operate locally).
5. **Criteria**: five CSFs plus justified local criteria (e.g. regulatory landscape; external confidence); indicators per CSF, tagged deal-breaker or differentiator; setup vs ongoing.
6. **Assess**: initial sift → strategic-fit gate → other CSFs → overall; working-group review with challenge round.
7. **Short list** (BAU, do minimum, preferred, more and less ambitious where applicable); summary matrix.
8. **Preferred option**: the basic choice often reduces to in-house vs new special-purpose vehicle; show what each offers. Then the rationale vs each alternative, and the risks that remain. Structure choices are usually hard to reverse: state what the preferred option keeps open and rules out, consider a **transition path** (e.g. hosted programme now, company later when a trigger is met: `steps/09-decide.md` §9.2), run a pre-mortem, and give switching values.
9. **Legal considerations** whatever the model (subsidy, procurement, funding powers, contracting, competition, aid rules), as questions for advisers.
10. **Sub-choices**: team, funding, name, hosting (`choices/design-choices.md`).

## Indicator starters (from practice)

These come from one kind of body (a public unit selling services abroad). Treat them as prompts and rewrite them for the user's purpose. Two things to add every time:
- **Sector rules that can rule a form out**: ask the user, and check, what legal powers, trading or charging rules, procurement rules, security or ethics guidance apply in their sector. These are usually the real deal-breakers.
- **Statutory duties** for UK public bodies (equality, biodiversity, environmental principles): record them in the Principles tab and check every short-listed option against them (`rulebooks/green-book.md`).

- *Strategic fit*: consistent with organisational values; has powers/remit; allows future flexibility; signals permission to act; signals new investment; can be a single front door; appetite for commercial risk; quick/agile; connected to overseas network; insulated from political change; aligned with and able to influence the wider agenda; meets stakeholder expectations; can involve all relevant nations/regions.
- *Value for money*: can contract with foreign governments; will pursue opportunities; can generate appropriate surplus; can carry surplus over; lower costs from existing infrastructure; can manage many/large/long projects; can materially increase returns; easy funding flows.
- *Supplier capacity/capability*: transparent accountability; meets governance requirements; likely positive reputation; clearly differentiated; balances centre and stakeholders; credible national offer; perceived as a public body.
- *Affordability*: low overheads; low cost to establish legal personality; existing budget for staff; lower deal cost; flexible funding / low cost of capital; potential fee income.
- *Achievability*: immediate access to budget; appetite to own in short term; time to recruit senior roles; time for legislation/legal set-up; time for stakeholder alignment; access to commercial expertise.
- *Regulatory* (local): no new legislation needed; supports risk appetite and investment; less constrained by competition/subsidy rules; can influence regulation and trade barriers; easier spending review; free to transact using multiple models.
- *External confidence* (local): not perceived as privatisation; seen as credible and professional; insulates core domestic responsibilities; clearly communicated; confidence in accountability; aligned to existing remit; matches buyers' preference; positive reputation at home and abroad.

## Outputs and storyline

Purpose and approach → method (six steps, scope of this pack) → principles → activity/operating/transaction framing → scope → long list (family tree) → criteria and sub-questions → roll-up rules → indicator matrix per CSF → summary matrix → short list → drop-out tracker → in-house vs new vehicle trade-off → rationale vs alternatives and residual risks → legal considerations → sub-choices → next steps. Annex: comparators, key differences between forms (accountability, oversight, funding, establishment, staffing, accounts), evidence base.

## Worked example (illustrative: a composite with details changed)

A multi-agency programme choosing a permanent home appraised about a dozen organisational forms in four families (public bodies, partnerships, not-for-profits, a company), after ruling out a few at the start. It used the five standard success factors plus two of its own (regulation and external confidence), each broken into six to a dozen statements, scored by a working group with a challenge round. Four reached the short list: two existing bodies (one as business as usual), a hosted joint initiative, and a new publicly owned company. The group preferred a joint unit hosted by an existing body. It beat the other hosts on existing infrastructure, international links and remit; beat business as usual by signalling new investment and moving faster; and beat a new company on links to policy, reassurance to buyers and lower set-up cost. The remaining risks were stated: how it would be perceived, complicated funding flows, and fit with the host's appetite for commercial work.

## Checklist
- [standard] Comparators researched; long list across families
- [standard] In-house vs new vehicle trade-off; transition path considered
- [standard] Legal considerations as questions for advisers
