# Type of choice: Who ("Who should we work with?")

Use for screening and prioritising organisations as partners, suppliers, providers, grantees, distributors, acquisition targets or co-investors. Also the default for generic "choose between named things" questions (vendors, venues, tools).

## Default method

**Knock-outs → scored criteria with defined levels → ranked tiers → one-page profiles** (`methods/scored-criteria.md`). For long lists with hard data on the key tests (more than about 12), put a threshold filter first (`methods/threshold-filter.md`).

**A small shortlist of products or vendors** (three CRMs, two venues): knock-outs, a few scored criteria with cost shown separately, and a clear recommendation in the reply. Skip the long list by source, batches, tiers and profiles.

## Steps

1. **Frame**: what the partnership is for, and what a good partner must bring. Agree templates (profile and spreadsheet) before researching.
2. **Long list by source**, kept as a visible grouping: e.g. (1) priorities named by the business, (2) organisations on an approved or accredited list, (3) organisations discovered during research, (4) others for future screening. The skill can search for group 3.
3. **Knock-outs**: e.g. disqualifying product-safety or contamination record, sanctions, serious unresolved ethics findings, conflicting exclusive arrangements. Decide up front how reputation issues count (knock-out, deal-breaker, or context).
4. **Criteria** (3–5) with level definitions. Starters:
   - **Vision / alignment**: how aligned the organisation is with our goals (e.g. committed to the target markets)
   - **Quality / capability**: confidence it can deliver to standard (systems, certifications, track record, recalls)
   - **Likely interest**: how keen it will be on this specific opportunity (size relative to its revenue, strategy, recent moves)
   - Optional: **scale / capacity**, **presence in target geography**, **commercial skills**, **cost**, **speed**
   - **For software, equipment or services**: fit with the main tasks (tested with the actual users if possible), ease of use for non-specialists, set-up effort and who does it, total cost over three to five years (licences, set-up, training, support, add-ons), integrations with what's already used, data protection and where data is held, support and the supplier's track record, and the cost of leaving.
   - **Sector-specific tests**: ask what's being bought (product, format, volumes, standards) and research what the sector requires: certification schemes, regulatory approvals, minimum order sizes, buyer qualification. These are often the real knock-outs.
5. **Research in batches** (priority group first), drafting profiles with sources and confidence (`steps/06-evidence.md`).
6. **Score, rank, tier**; show the segment view if organisations fall into types.
7. **Review** with the group; resolve unknowns through outreach.
8. **Recommend** front-runners and next steps per organisation.

## Profile ("organisation on a page") fields

- **Header**: name, legal name and listing (or "private"), type tag, badges (e.g. on an approved list).
- **Information box**: HQ, founded, revenue (and in the relevant segment, with calculation in the notes), employees, business segments (relevant one marked).
- **Scores**: per criterion and total, each with confidence.
- **Evaluation**: 1–3 evidence bullets per criterion, plus **Other** (reputation, controversies with outcome and date, notable facts).
- **Nice-to-haves**: extra value beyond the criteria.
- **Next steps**: e.g. reach out to explore interest; specific diligence; technical team to investigate an approach.
- **Notes**: highlights (pros, caveat) and sources.

## Outputs and storyline

Executive summary (results; approach; observations such as splits between types) → long list by source → timeline and templates (if the screening is staged) → methodology (research, criteria, qualitative factors) → ranked summary of the top tier → pros/cons of the top tier → others → needs further research → excluded with reasons → profiles. Annex: origins of the long list.

## Worked example (illustrative: a composite with details changed)

A nonprofit screened a few dozen potential manufacturing partners from several sources (internal priorities, an accredited-supplier list, desk research) in weekly batches. Criteria: shared vision, quality and likely interest, each scored 0–3. A handful of front-runners emerged, and the clearest finding was a split between two quite different types of manufacturer. Public information thinned out sharply beyond the largest firms. Two lessons are built into this skill: one firm was called an "automatic disqualification" on quality yet still ranked (use knock-outs instead), and some firms lost points for missing information (record low confidence instead).

## Checklist
- [quick] Long list by source; templates agreed before research
- [standard] Research in batches; unverifiable fields marked 'unverified'
