# Type of choice: Scope ("What's in and out?")

Use when the user needs to agree the boundaries of an initiative before (or while) choosing what to do and how: which sectors, organisation types, funding types, geographies, markets and offers are priorities now, which have major potential, and which are for later.

## Default method

**Scope spectrum by facet**, agreed through engagement, with a rationale per facet. No scoring is needed unless the group disagrees; if it does, use scored criteria (`methods/scored-criteria.md`) on the contested facet only, or a threshold filter for markets/geographies (see `choices/where.md`).

1. Pick the **facets** that define scope for this initiative. Starter set (adapt):
   - **Sector**: e.g. public / private partners / other public-sector partners (academia, professional bodies)
   - **Organisation type**: e.g. front-line providers / arm's-length bodies / wider family
   - **Financial / funding**: e.g. direct income generation / aid-funded / other-funded / philanthropy
   - **Geography of the supply side**: e.g. one nation first, others by engagement
   - **Market** (where the work is sold or delivered): regions or countries
   - **Offer**: the service lines or products
2. For each facet, lay out the **spectrum of options** and mark each with one of three levels: **initial priority** / **area of major potential (ongoing engagement)** / **potential future scope** (connections made as appropriate).
3. Write a **rationale** per facet: why the line is where it is, what engagement is still under way, and any principle it follows (e.g. cost-neutral after full costs).
4. Record what would move an item between levels.

## Criteria starters (if scoring is needed)

Strategic alignment; demand evidence; ability to deliver now; risk (legal, reputational); cost to serve; dependency on others.

## Outputs

- **Scope spectrum slide**: rows = facets, spectrum of options as pills coloured by level, legend, rationale column, footnotes.
- Optional **"answer on a page"** once other types of choice are done: scope, activity, phasing, operating model, team, funding, each in two or three bullets.

## Pitfalls

- Treating scope as settled when key stakeholders haven't agreed: mark "subject to review pending engagement" in the headline.
- Mixing scope (what is covered) with function (what the unit does): keep the latter for `choices/what.md`.

## Worked example (illustrative: a composite with details changed)

A new public unit set up to sell its sector's expertise abroad set its scope along six dimensions: partners, channels, funding types, home regions, overseas markets and offers. It sorted each into three bands. **Priorities now**: public providers, direct income, one home region, a handful of overseas markets, and three core offers. **Major potential**: private partners, aid-funded work, the other home regions, one more market cluster, and two newer offers. **Later**: other public bodies, philanthropy, licensing. A later version added two dimensions as engagement taught the team what mattered. Scope pages are expected to change over time.

## Checklist
- [quick] Facets chosen; options marked initial priority / major potential / future; rationale per facet
