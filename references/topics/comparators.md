# Comparators and precedents: "how have others done this?"

Comparator research informs the long list (options you hadn't considered), the criteria (what made others succeed or fail), and the ratings (precedents that test a judgement). Use it when the choice is unusual for the organisation, when stakeholders will ask "who else does this?", or when a rating hinges on whether something has worked before.

## Method

1. **Define the comparison set**: peers in the same system (other departments, other trusts, other firms), international equivalents, and adjacent sectors that solved the same problem. Aim for 5–15; more becomes a census.
2. **Fix the attributes before researching**, so every entry is described the same way. Typical attributes:
   - offering / what it does
   - legal or organisational model (e.g. limited company established by legislation, joint venture, public institution, agency)
   - size (revenue, staff, contracts, countries)
   - principal remit
   - accountability (who it answers to)
   - funding (government, multilateral, transaction fees, mixed)
   - what is transferable to us / "notes for us"
3. **Classify** entries along one or two meaningful dimensions (e.g. centralised and sector-agnostic vs devolved and focused; acquisition vehicle vs service provider). This turns a list into insight.
4. **Extract lessons**: what made them work, what didn't, what conditions they depended on that we may not have.

## Outputs

- **Comparator table**: one column per comparator, one row per attribute; a final "notes for us" row.
- **Attribute tick matrix**: rows = attributes grouped (remit / operating model / accountability / funding), columns = comparators, ticks where they apply. Shows patterns at a glance ("only one vehicle used the private sector to deliver").
- **Option cards with examples**: for each option family, columns for *benefits / weaknesses / examples / recommendation*. The recommendation says whether to **include or exclude the option from the appraisal, and why**: this is where initial-sift reasoning is written down.
- **Hosting/combination grid**: options × hosts (or other second dimension), with the pros/cons of each pairing and a recommendation row.

## Cautions

- Comparators prove possibility, not suitability: say what differs about their context.
- Figures from comparators' own publications are claims; date and source them.
- Don't let one vivid case dominate; note how many comparators support each lesson.
