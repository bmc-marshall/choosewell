# Engagement: answering the open questions with people

Most appraisals reach a point where the evidence can only come from people: whether partners would take part, whether the regulator sees a problem, whether buyers would trust a vehicle. Engagement is also how the result gains legitimacy. Use this module to turn open questions into a plan and to document the evidence base.

## 1. Map who matters, by role

| Role | What they give | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Sponsors** | Direction, sign-off, funding | Executive steering group, ministers, board |
| **Stakeholders** | Must be consulted for success; can block | Organisations whose remit changes |
| **Co-developers** | Early feedback and testing | Special interest groups, practitioner networks |
| **Potential partners** | Shared delivery; also assessed as options | Providers, suppliers, peer bodies |
| **External references** | Lessons and precedents | Comparable bodies elsewhere, academics |

Organisations can hold several roles; a landscape map (grouped by type and tier) helps people see the whole picture.

## 2. Plan by aim, not by meeting

Typical aims in sequence:
1. **Early steer**: is the direction right? (sponsors, senior stakeholders)
2. **Awareness**: let the wider landscape know it's happening (conferences, briefings, public materials)
3. **Test and iterate**: test functions, criteria, options, ratings (focus groups, workshops, surveys)
4. **Build momentum**: launch planning and communications

For each open question in the workbook, assign: who can answer, through which channel (interview, focus group, survey, workshop, formal advice, written request), by when, and what answer would change which rating. Questions that would change the result come first.

## 3. Channels and what they're good for

- **Interviews / targeted conversations**: depth, sensitive views, senior people.
- **Focus groups and special interest groups**: testing ideas against practitioners; generating counts of mentions (useful for countable criteria such as "impact = number of independent mentions").
- **Surveys**: breadth; prioritisation across many organisations; statements of intent.
- **Workshops**: scoring sessions, co-design, resolving disagreements.
- **Formal advice**: legal, financial, regulatory.
- **Research reports**: commissioned or existing studies; count inclusion as evidence where relevant.

## 4. The evidence-base slide

Close the appraisal with a slide showing the analysis behind each stage, so readers trust the result. Structure: stages down the side (e.g. engagement → scope & ambition → function → form (long list) → form (short list) → implementation), with the evidence for each: number of meetings and organisations, focus groups, research used, legal input, surveys, workshop hours, comparators, pilots, modelling. Footnote each item to its source document. An optional "iteration" arrow shows the stages were revisited, not done once.
