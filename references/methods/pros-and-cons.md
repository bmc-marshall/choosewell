# Method: pros and cons with option labels

Use for a single design decision with two to four options: team set-up, funding method, hosting body, name, location of a unit. It is a lightweight appraisal: quick, readable, and honest about trade-offs, without scoring.

## Steps

1. **State the deciding criterion first.** Most small decisions have one consideration that matters most ("the priority is not matching the name to the function, but a name that works overseas and keeps public confidence"). Say it in the subtitle; it stops the pros/cons from becoming a list contest.
2. **Apply principles as knock-outs.** Rules that must be met (brand rules, legal constraints, budget caps) filter the options before pros and cons. List the principles.
3. **Describe each option in one line** (what it is in practice).
4. **Pros and cons**, parallel and specific, with ✓ / ✗ markers. Keep the lists comparable in length and level; one-word pros against paragraph-long cons reads as bias.
5. **Label the options** using appraisal language: **Preferred option**, **Do minimum**, **Do nothing** (or BAU), **Alternative**. This links small decisions back to the main appraisal and shows the benchmark was considered.
6. **Recommend**, with the reason in one line, and note what further evidence (testing, modelling, legal view) would confirm it ("further modelling can quantify income under each option").
7. **Say how reversible it is.** Many design choices are two-way doors (a team structure can change next year): decide quickly and set a review point. If it's a one-way door (a public name, a legal form), slow down: adverse consequences for the top two, and consider a staged or trial version.

## Extra steps for naming and branding choices

- Principles (e.g. the parent organisation's brand rules: written in full, includes required identifiers, geographic reference rules, partnership vs organisation vs service naming).
- **Lexicon of existing names** in the landscape, to avoid clashes and confusion with past or current initiatives.
- **How comparable bodies chose** their names (research or conversations).
- **User testing** with the people the name must work for (partners, overseas posts, comms teams, external stakeholders).
- Then pros/cons and the shortlisted recommendation for the decision-maker.

## Outputs

- **Option cards**: three or four columns, label above each (Preferred / Do minimum / Do nothing), one-line description, ✓ pros, ✗ cons, preferred option outlined.
- **Option-structure diagrams** where the options are organisational (e.g. ringfenced team vs distributed team vs integrated team under the same head).
- **Pros/cons table** for longer lists (option / pros / cons, recommended row marked).

## Checklist
- [quick] Deciding criterion stated first
- [quick] Principles applied as knock-outs
- [quick] Parallel pros/cons; labels Preferred / Do minimum / Do nothing / Alternative
- [quick] Reversibility stated; review point set for two-way doors
