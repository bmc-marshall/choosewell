# Step 2: Set the rules and rigour (Tree 2)

Decide which rulebook applies, how thorough to be, and how carefully to document the work. This looks only at the setting, not at the options.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skip if | Never. It takes minutes and stops a quick screen being mistaken for a business case |
| You'll end up with | A rulebook, a depth and a defensibility setting, recorded on the frame |
| Next | Step 3; or, if analysis can't answer the question yet, a pilot or staged decision (§2.4) |

## 2.1 Who is the decision for? (rulebook)

| Setting | Rulebook |
|---|---|
| UK central government | `rulebooks/green-book.md` |
| US federal government | `rulebooks/us-federal.md` |
| Anyone else (charities, companies, local and devolved government, international bodies) | `rulebooks/general-method.md` |

Say which applies and why, in a sentence. If the user says "our government" without naming it, use the general method and ask which country; the UK and US rulebooks apply only to their own central governments, but other governments' own appraisal rules may apply and should be checked.

## 2.2 How reversible, and how much is at stake? (depth)

| | Easy to reverse | Hard to reverse |
|---|---|---|
| **Low stakes** | Quick: decide fast | Standard; look for flexible versions (Step 3) |
| **High stakes** | Standard: decide, monitor, correct | Full: deliberate; scenarios; independent review |

Make reversible decisions quickly and irreversible ones carefully and late (Bezos's "two-way and one-way doors"). For reversible decisions, acting with most of the information you'd like is usually better than waiting for all of it.

| Depth | What it involves (each level adds to the one above) |
|---|---|
| Quick | A few criteria with written score definitions; a check of what single change would alter the answer; sources for every fact |
| Standard | Knock-outs; private scoring before group discussion; price compared separately; switching values; prioritised open questions; what could go wrong |
| Full | A structured long list; costed comparison of the short list; scenarios; independent review |

Recommend a depth and let the user overrule it. A user's deadline counts: if they need an answer by Friday, work at the depth the time allows and say in one line what that leaves out (for example, no private scoring, one source per criterion). For a UK business case, the depth follows the stage: strategic outline case (long list, success factors, preferred way forward) at standard; outline and full business cases (costed short list) at full.

## 2.3 Will it be scrutinised? (defensibility)

If an auditor, regulator, board, funder or the public may examine the decision, it must be **defensible**: pass/fail tests, a full decisions log, sources for every claim. Public money and board decisions are usually "yes". Tree 3 (Step 4) uses this answer.

## 2.4 Can analysis answer it now?

Some problems only reveal cause and effect in hindsight ("complex" problems in the Cynefin framework). Scoring options in advance will mislead there.

| Kind of problem | Signs | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Clear | Known answer, stable | Apply known practice; no appraisal needed |
| Complicated | Answerable with analysis or expertise | Carry on |
| Complex | Experts disagree about what would even work; the situation is shifting | Pilot or stage the decision; appraise once patterns emerge |
| Chaotic | Crisis | Act to stabilise first |

If piloting is itself one of the options being weighed ("should we pilot first?"), it isn't a reason to stop: appraise it as an option, usually with a quantified tree (`methods/quantified-tree.md` §7–8).

If unsure, split the problem: parts may sit in different categories.

## Checklist
- [quick] Rulebook chosen (UK Green Book, US federal, or general method) and the reason stated
- [quick] Depth set from reversibility and stakes, and recorded
- [quick] Defensibility set: will it be scrutinised?
- [quick] Checked whether analysis can answer it now; if not, pilot or stage first
- [quick] Method choices explained to the user in plain language: what, why, implications, source, main alternative
