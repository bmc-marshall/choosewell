# Step 6: Gather the evidence

Find out what's true about each option, recording where each fact came from and how sure you are. A clearly marked gap is far more useful than a confident guess.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skip if | Never; at quick depth one source per criterion is enough |
| You'll end up with | Evidence and sources against every rating, a claims log, and open questions (workbook Ratings, Claims and Open questions tabs) |
| Next | Step 7 |

## 6.1 Depth and order of research

- **Depth** follows Step 2: quick (headline facts, one source per criterion), standard (two or three sources per criterion, financials, controversies), full (annual reports, filings, five years of news). A human analyst spends 30 minutes to 2 hours per organisation on a standard screening profile; use that as a guide to depth, not time.
- **Source order**: (1) the user's own material, which may contradict public sources; (2) primary sources: annual reports, regulatory filings, official statistics, government and regulator sites, the organisation's own site; (3) reputable news and sector bodies; (4) encyclopaedic summaries for orientation only, following through to their cited sources.
- **Long lists (30+)**: research in batches, priority group first, and share early results so the group can redirect.
- **Sources with a stake**: figures from a vendor, a reseller, a bidder or a party to a dispute are claims, not facts. Log who said it, look for an independent source for anything that drives the result, and lower the confidence if there isn't one.
- **Prices, discounts and eligibility vary by country and sector** (nonprofit discounts, regional pricing, tax reliefs): check them for the user's location, and say which location you assumed.
- **Money in different currencies or years**: convert to one currency and price base, and record the rate and date used as an estimate in the claims log.
- **Inaccessible sources** (LinkedIn headcounts, paid company databases, paywalled news): don't guess. Mark the field **unverified** and list it as a quick check for the user.

## 6.2 Recording: the claims log

Every factual claim that feeds a rating, profile or headline is recorded once:

| Field | Values |
|---|---|
| Claim | One specific fact ("2024 revenue £32m") |
| Type | **Fact** (primary source) / **Claim** (the subject says so about itself) / **Inference** (our reasoning) / **Estimate** (a number we derived, with the calculation) |
| Source | Link, or document and page; for inferences, the facts they rest on |
| Date | Of the information; flag anything over ~3 years old for fast-moving facts |
| Confidence | High (direct, recent, reliable) / Medium (indirect or dated) / Low (inference or thin) |
| Checked | Who or what verified it, and when (filled in at Step 10) |

No source, no fact: unsourced statements become inferences or unknowns, and are labelled as such wherever they appear, including on slides. Controversies are recorded with what happened, when, the outcome and the source; their weight was decided in Step 5.1.

## 6.3 Unknowns, drafts and estimates

- **Missing information lowers confidence, not the score.** Leave the score blank, mark it low confidence, and note the "best case if it resolves well" so promising-but-obscure options aren't buried. Docking points mixes "weak" with "unknown".
- Each important unknown becomes an **open question** with who could answer it and through which channel (`topics/engagement.md`).
- **Drafted content**: anything the skill drafts (a rating, a profile, a score definition) is marked **[Drafted - for group review]** in the workbook and on any slide until the group confirms it. This is what makes substantial drafting safe while the group stays in charge.
- **Reality-check estimates**: before accepting a cost, timeline or take-up estimate, ask how similar projects actually turned out (reference-class forecasting, the evidence base behind the Green Book's optimism-bias adjustment).
- **Probabilities** are evidence too: log each one as an estimate with its basis (records of similar cases, judgement, or both) and a low and high value. How to ask for them well is in `methods/quantified-tree.md` §4.
- **Formal evidence** (legal advice, a published strategy, audited figures) outranks judgement; record which a rating rests on.

## Checklist
- [quick] Every fact has a source; every rating has a confidence level
- [quick] Unknowns logged as open questions with an owner, not scored down
- [quick] Drafted content labelled [Drafted - for group review]
- [standard] Claims log kept for the claims that drive the result (type, source, date, confidence)
- [standard] Inaccessible fields marked unverified rather than guessed
- [standard] Cost, time and take-up estimates checked against similar projects
